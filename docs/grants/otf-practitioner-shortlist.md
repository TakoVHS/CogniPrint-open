# OTF practitioner discovery shortlist

Status: outreach candidates for problem discovery only. Inclusion does **not** imply endorsement, partnership, interest, or participation in CogniPrint.

Selection date: 2026-07-25.

## Selection principle

Prioritise organisations whose public work already combines at least two of:

- human-rights or independent-media workflows;
- censorship, surveillance, or repressive information environments;
- verification/investigation of digital information;
- sensitive evidence handling;
- manipulated/synthetic media or provenance;
- open-source tooling and reproducible methods.

The first outreach should be small and sequential. Do not mass-email the list.

## Priority 1 — WITNESS, Technology Threats & Opportunities

Public fit:

- WITNESS works with activists, journalists, fact-checkers, and human-rights defenders on trust and documentation in an AI-mediated information environment.
- Its Technology Threats & Opportunities team specifically studies generative AI, manipulated media, provenance, and frontline needs.
- Jacobo Castellanos' public role includes AI/manipulated-media impacts and C2PA provenance work.
- Mahsa Alimardani's public role includes digital repression, censorship/surveillance, generative AI, and high-risk civil-society environments.

Why ask them:

CogniPrint's proposed boundary — content-derived signals are hypotheses while authenticated provenance is a separate evidence class — overlaps directly with WITNESS' public AI/provenance concerns. Their most valuable response would be to explain where a text-focused local evidence layer would be irrelevant, unsafe, or incomplete for frontline workflows.

Public references:

- https://www.gen-ai.witness.org/
- https://www.witness.org/portfolio_page/jacobo-castellanos/
- https://www.witness.org/portfolio_page/mahsa-alimardani/

Public contact channels previously published by WITNESS for Technology Threats & Opportunities work include `sam@witness.org` and `raquel@witness.org`. Re-check current role/contact status before sending.

Suggested first question:

> In your frontline consultations, are there situations involving contested text where local content-derived evidence would actually help, or is the important provenance problem predominantly elsewhere? What would make such a tool unsafe?

## Priority 2 — Mnemonic

Public fit:

- Mnemonic archives, verifies, preserves, and investigates digital information documenting human-rights violations and international crimes.
- It operates stand-alone archives for high-risk contexts and handles confidential/time-sensitive research requests.
- It develops and supports open-source tools and methods for human-rights defenders, journalists, investigators, and accountability work.

Why ask them:

Mnemonic can test whether CogniPrint's proposed split between observed content evidence and authenticated provenance corresponds to a real evidence-handling need. They can also identify chain-of-custody, disclosure, retention, and false-attribution risks that an AI-focused project may otherwise miss.

Public references:

- https://mnemonic.org/
- https://mnemonic.org/en/about/methods/
- https://mnemonic.org/en/about/contact-us/

Public general contact: `info@mnemonic.org`.

Suggested first question:

> When digital documentation is contested or transformed, is there any useful role for a local statistical text profile alongside your existing provenance/verification methods, and what evidentiary boundary would be mandatory?

## Priority 3 — Qurium Media Foundation / digital forensics

Public fit:

- Qurium performs digital-forensics investigations involving internet censorship, disinformation campaigns, malware, attacks against independent media, and other threats to a free and open internet.
- It collaborates with independent media and investigative journalists.
- Its public investigations include cloned-media/disinformation infrastructure and censorship-related attacks.

Why ask them:

Qurium can test whether the proposed tool would add anything to actual forensic triage, or whether infrastructure/network evidence is substantially more important than text fingerprints in their cases. A negative answer would be highly valuable for the OTF GO/NO-GO decision.

Public references:

- https://www.qurium.org/alerts/
- https://www.qurium.org/forensic-disinformation/

Public digital-forensics contact published in Qurium reports: Tord Lundström, `t@virtualroad.org`.

Suggested first question:

> In investigations of cloned media or coordinated disinformation, is content-level text evidence ever a meaningful triage signal, or would it create more false confidence than value compared with infrastructure and provenance evidence?

## Threat-model reference — Access Now Digital Security Helpline

Access Now's Helpline publicly supports civil society, journalists, bloggers, researchers, activists, and human-rights defenders facing surveillance, censorship, account compromise, DDoS, and other digital-security threats.

It is useful as a **threat-model reference**, but the Helpline should not be treated as a free project-review service or research panel. Do not send a generic CogniPrint discovery request to the emergency-support address unless Access Now indicates that such contact is appropriate.

Public references:

- https://www.accessnow.org/help/
- https://www.accessnow.org/help/helpline-services/

## Outreach sequence

1. WITNESS — strongest overlap with synthetic media/provenance/frontline consultations.
2. Mnemonic — strongest evidence-chain and confidential documentation perspective.
3. Qurium — strongest independent-media digital-forensics/censorship perspective.

Stop and reassess after every response. A substantive NO or "this is not useful" should change the project rather than trigger more outreach designed to find someone who agrees.

## What counts as useful discovery evidence

Useful responses include criticism such as:

- "we would never upload this material to a third party";
- "model attribution is not a decision we make";
- "the provenance field we actually need is X";
- "false positives would endanger a source";
- "this would only be useful before publication, not as evidence";
- "we need Arabic/Russian/Persian/etc. before this matters";
- "existing tool Y already covers this";
- "the whole text-fingerprint premise is not useful in our workflow."

Generic encouragement does not satisfy practitioner discovery.
