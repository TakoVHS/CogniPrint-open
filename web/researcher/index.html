Research story

Roman Adriashkin develops CogniPrint as an independent research programme at the intersection of applied mathematics, statistical language measurement, reproducible software and AI evidence. The work began with a deliberately interpretable question: whether text transformations can be described as changes in a compact finite-dimensional profile.

That question led to a 12-dimensional feature map, Euclidean and cosine geometry, entropy and n-gram diagnostics, controlled perturbation campaigns, benchmark comparisons and a manuscript on cognitive fingerprints as mathematical text profiles.

The empirical programme also produced a corrective result: on a fixed Stage A classification task, transparent TF-IDF baselines outperform the current 12D nearest-centroid profile. Rather than hiding that result, the programme uses it to narrow the role of the profile and strengthen the distinction between measurement and attribution.

The current broader research direction asks how evidence can remain portable and auditable in AI-mediated workflows — including multi-principal provenance, failure tracing, privacy-preserving verification and explicit UNKNOWN states.

Positioning note: “independent mathematical researcher” describes the research activity and programme. This page does not assert an academic degree, faculty appointment or institutional affiliation that has not been independently verified.
Research philosophy

Mathematics is most useful here not when it creates certainty, but when it tells us exactly what was measured — and where certainty must stop.

Interpretability

Prefer measurable coordinates and transparent baselines before opaque claims.

Reproducibility

Bind results to code, data lineage, hashes and explicit execution states.

Scientific restraint

Preserve uncertainty, negative results and claim boundaries as first-class research outputs.
